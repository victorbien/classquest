1 About this guide

This is the end-user manual for ClassQuest, a lightweight online course platform. It explains how to sign in, how each kind of user works day to day, and how to get the system running on your own machine for a demonstration.

ClassQuest has three kinds of users:

  • Students browse published courses and open their resources (documents, digital books and videos), and track which ones they have opened.
  • Teachers create courses, upload resources into them, and publish courses so students can see them. Teachers also have access to an Operations screen for demonstrations.
  • Administrators monitor the health of the system, run the same operations controls, and browse every resource in the library.

This is a research prototype. All content is synthetic sample data, and the system is meant to be run locally for demonstration. It is not a production service and does not store real student records, grades or personal data.

2 Key concepts

A few terms are used throughout this guide.

  • Course — a container for related learning resources. A course has a title, a description, a category, an optional cover image, and a status.
  • Resource — a single learning item inside a course. There are three types: Document, Book (a digital book) and Video. Each resource can carry an optional week or section label.
  • Course status — a course is one of:
    • Draft — only its teacher (and administrators) can see it. Students cannot.
    • Published — visible to students.
    • Archived — hidden from students again; its resources and history are kept, but no new resources can be added.
  • Resource processing — when a resource is uploaded, it goes through a short automatic pipeline (Submitted → Queued → Processing → Completed) before it is ready. This usually takes a few seconds. Students only ever see resources that have reached Completed.
  • My Progress — for students, this means resources opened. It is a record of what you have opened, overall and per course. It is not a grade, a score, or a measure of mastery or lesson completion.

3 Getting started

3.1 Signing in

  1. Open ClassQuest in your web browser at http://localhost:8080.
  2. Enter your email and password on the Login screen.
  3. Select Sign in. You land on the home screen for your role:
    • Students land on Home.
    • Teachers land on their Dashboard.
    • Administrators land on Operations.

3.1.1 Demonstration accounts

When the prototype runs in demo mode (the default), three accounts are created for you automatically. These are public, synthetic credentials for demonstration only.

Role Email Password
Student student@classquest.example DemoStudent123!
Teacher teacher@classquest.example DemoTeacher123!
Administrator admin@classquest.example DemoAdmin123!

3.2 Finding your way around

Every screen shares the same layout:

  • A sidebar on the left with the pages available to your role.
  • A top bar showing the current page and your account, with the option to sign out.

The pages you see depend on your role:

Role Pages in the sidebar
Student Home · Courses · My Progress
Teacher Dashboard · My Courses · Publish Resource · Operations
Administrator Operations · Library

If you try to open a page that is not meant for your role, ClassQuest simply returns you to your own home page.

3.3 Signing out

Use the account control in the top bar to sign out. Your session is secured with a sign-in token that expires automatically after about an hour, so you may be asked to sign in again during a long session.

4 Student guide

As a student you can browse published courses, open their resources, and see your own progress. You cannot see draft or archived courses.

4.1 Home

The Home screen is your starting point. When you have opened at least one resource, it shows a Continue where you left off shortcut so you can jump straight back to recent material.

4.2 Browsing courses

  1. Select Courses in the sidebar.
  2. You see every published course as a card, with its cover image (or a coloured placeholder), title, category, and your own progress for that course (for example 3 / 4 resources opened · 75%).
  3. Select a course to open it.

Draft and archived courses are never listed for students, so the list only ever shows material a teacher has deliberately published.

4.3 Opening a resource

Inside a course:

  1. Resources are grouped by their week or section label and shown in the order the teacher arranged them.
  2. Only completed resources appear. If a teacher has just uploaded something, it shows up once processing finishes.
  3. Select Open on a resource. ClassQuest opens the file in a new browser tab using a secure, time-limited link (valid for about five minutes). If you leave the tab open for a long time and the link expires, simply select Open again for a fresh link.

4.4 Tracking your progress

Select My Progress in the sidebar to see:

  • Your overall totals — how many resources you have opened out of how many are available to you.
  • A coverage percentage.
  • A breakdown by resource type (documents, books, videos).
  • A breakdown by course, each with opened / available and a percentage.
  • A Recently opened list.

Remember: progress counts a resource the moment you open it. It reflects what you have looked at, not how well you understood it. Only completed resources in published courses are counted, so your “opened” total can never exceed your “available” total.

5 Teacher guide

As a teacher you create courses, add resources, arrange and publish them, and manage each course through its lifecycle. You can only manage courses you created.

5.1 Dashboard

The Dashboard is your landing page. It summarises recent activity and the processing pipeline, and includes a My Courses card focused on the courses you own.

5.2 Creating a course

  1. Select My Courses → Create Course.
  2. Fill in:
    • Title
    • Description
    • Category
    • Cover image (optional) — a PNG, JPEG or WebP image up to 2 MB. If you do not provide one, ClassQuest draws a category-tinted placeholder.
  3. Select Save as draft. The course is created in Draft status, so students cannot see it yet.

5.3 Adding a resource

Open one of your courses, then:

  1. Select Add Resource.
  2. Choose the type: Document, Book or Video.
  3. Enter a title, an optional description, and an optional week or section label (used to group resources on the course page).
  4. Drag and drop a file, or browse for one. Keep demonstration files small.
  5. Select Upload & publish.

A Processing pipeline indicator moves through Submitted → Queued → Processing → Completed, usually within a few seconds. When it reaches Completed, the resource joins the course’s ordered list.

Only the file type and size are checked. If a file is rejected, nothing is stored and you see a short message explaining why — just try a different file.

5.4 Arranging resources

On the course page you can:

  • Reorder resources with the up/down arrows. New resources are added to the end of the list.
  • Edit a resource’s details inline.

5.5 Publishing, archiving and restoring a course

A course moves through a simple lifecycle. Use the controls on the course page (or Edit Course):

  • Publish Course — moves a draft to published. Students can now see the course and its completed resources.
  • Return to draft — moves a published course back to draft, hiding it from students again.
  • Archive course — hides the course from students immediately. Its resources and access history are kept, but you cannot add new resources while it is archived.
  • Restore to draft — brings an archived course back to draft so you can work on it again. (An archived course cannot go straight back to published; restore it to draft first, then publish.)

Allowed transitions at a glance:

From You can go to
Draft Published, Archived
Published Draft, Archived
Archived Draft

5.6 A note on what courses do and do not include

Courses are deliberately lightweight. There is no enrolment — every student sees every published course. There are no grades, quizzes, assignments, forums or certificates. Resource ordering uses the up/down arrows rather than drag-and-drop.

6 Administrator guide

Administrators focus on the health of the system and on browsing the full library. Administrators can also manage any course (not just their own) through the system’s interface.

6.1 Operations

The Operations screen is the control centre for a demonstration. It shows the live state of the system and provides the demonstration controls described in Demonstration controls.

Key panels:

  • Service health — the status of each underlying service the system depends on (database, object storage, message queue, monitoring and alerting). If something is down, it shows here.
  • Storage overview — how resource files are distributed across storage tiers.
  • Monitoring — the count of recent bad (HTTP 400) requests and the monitoring alarm state.

6.2 Library

The Library gives administrators a view of every resource across all courses, which is useful for an overview or spot checks. (Teachers also have a library view limited to the all-resources list; students instead use Courses.)

7 Demonstration controls

The Operations screen (available to teachers and administrators) includes a set of Demonstration Controls. These exist to show how the system behaves; everything they create is clearly labelled as demo/sample content so students never see it by accident.

Control What it does
Seed demo catalogue Creates four sample courses — Cloud Computing, Applied Blockchain, Data Analytics (published) and Cybersecurity Essentials (draft) — and uploads eleven resources through the normal pipeline. Running it again skips anything that already exists.
Induce processing failure Deliberately makes one resource fail processing to show automatic retries. After the third attempt the resource is marked Failed and the message is moved to a dead-letter queue. The failing resource stays in a hidden draft course, so students never see it.
Simulate Glacier tiering Moves up to ten completed demo resources to the long-term “Glacier” storage tier immediately, instead of waiting for the normal 90-day rule.
Generate HTTP 400 burst Sends a burst of deliberately bad requests to demonstrate monitoring. The monitoring panel switches to Threshold exceeded and shows the count.

Local demonstration limits. When running locally, a few cloud behaviours are simulated rather than fully reproduced: the monitoring alarm is not expected to change to the fired state, alert emails are never actually sent, and Glacier restore is not implemented (so opening a resource that has been moved to the Glacier tier may be refused — which is how real long-term storage behaves without a restore step). These are expected and are labelled in the interface.

8 Running ClassQuest locally

This section is for the person setting up a demonstration. It summarises the quickest path to a running system. For full detail, Windows PowerShell commands and troubleshooting, see the companion document docs/LOCAL_SETUP.md; for a scripted walkthrough, see DEMO.md.

8.1 What you need

  • Docker Desktop (with Docker Compose), running.
  • Node.js 20 or later — only needed on the host to run the automated tests and helper scripts; the application itself runs inside Docker.
  • The network ports 8080, 4000, 3306 and 4566 free.
  • No AWS account and no cloud cost — the cloud services are emulated locally.

8.2 Start the stack

From the project folder:

# 1. Create your local settings file (defaults work as-is)
cp .env.example .env          # Windows PowerShell: Copy-Item .env.example .env

# 2. Build and start everything
docker compose up -d --build

# 3. Wait until the app-tier and web-tier show "healthy"
docker compose ps

The first build takes a few minutes. Then open http://localhost:8080 and sign in with one of the demonstration accounts.

8.3 Seed the sample content

With the stack running, create the sample courses and resources either from the interface (Operations → Demonstration Controls → Seed demo catalogue, as a teacher or administrator) or from the command line:

npm run demo:seed

8.4 Stop or reset

docker compose down        # stop, but keep all data
docker compose down -v     # stop and DELETE all data (fresh start next time)

Warning: docker compose down -v permanently deletes the local database and stored files. Use plain docker compose down if you want to keep your demo data for next time.

9 Troubleshooting

Symptom Likely cause and fix
The page will not load at localhost:8080 The stack may still be starting. Run docker compose ps and wait until app-tier and web-tier are healthy.
“Docker daemon” or “engine” errors Docker Desktop is not running. Start it and wait for Engine running, then start the stack again.
A container will not start because a port is “already allocated” Another program is using port 8080, 4000, 3306 or 4566 (often a local MySQL on 3306). Stop that program or free the port. See docs/LOCAL_SETUP.md for exact commands.
A student cannot see a course The course is in Draft or Archived status, or its resources have not finished processing. Publish the course and wait for resources to reach Completed.
A resource shows but will not open The download link may have expired (they last about five minutes) — select Open again. If the resource was moved to the Glacier tier during a demo, it may be refused by design.
A newly uploaded resource is missing Processing may still be running. Wait a few seconds for Submitted → Queued → Processing → Completed, or check the pipeline indicator.
The monitoring alarm never “fires” during a demo Expected when running locally — the local emulator does not evaluate the alarm. The request count still updates and is labelled accordingly.
You were signed out unexpectedly Sign-in tokens expire after about an hour. Simply sign in again.

10 Frequently asked questions

Is my data real? No. ClassQuest is a prototype that uses only synthetic sample content. It is intended for demonstration, not for managing real courses or student records.

Does “My Progress” mean my grade? No. Progress records which resources you have opened. It is not a grade, score, or measure of understanding or completion.

Why can’t students enrol in a specific course? By design there is no enrolment. Every student can see every published course.

Can a teacher edit another teacher’s course? No. Teachers manage only the courses they created. Administrators can manage any course.

Where can I learn how the system is built? See the project’s ARCHITECTURE.md and README.md. Those documents describe the technical design and how the prototype maps to a full cloud deployment.
